01 Getting out of a playground

Inside a playground there is no back button and no menu. The only control is the pair of corners, labelled along the bottom edge. That is the entire point of the app. There is exactly one way out, and it is designed so that an adult can do it in the dark and a one-year-old cannot do it at all.

  1. Put one finger in the bottom-left corner of the screen.
  2. Put another finger in the bottom-right corner, at the same time.
  3. Hold both. Each corner darkens, and the bar between them fills as it counts.
  4. After three seconds, the playground closes and you are back at the picker.

Two adult hands, two far-apart targets, one deliberate wait. Two thumbs, three seconds, you’re out.

If it is not working

  • Both corners, not one. Holding one corner does nothing, forever.
  • Do not lift. The count restarts if either finger leaves the glass.
  • Get right into the corners. The targets are the two circles at the bottom edge, and the area that responds is larger than the circle you can see.
  • A thick case can eat the corners. If your case has a raised lip, come in from the side rather than pressing down from directly above.
  • A wet or sticky screen confuses any touchscreen. Wipe it and try again.
  • Still stuck? Swipe up from the bottom edge to go Home, or press the side button to lock the phone. Baby Mode is an app, not a kidnapper.

Why not a passcode? Because you would be typing it forty times a day with one hand while holding a baby with the other. The gesture is faster than a passcode and just as impossible for a toddler. If you want something a determined four-year-old genuinely cannot beat, use Guided Access — next section.

02 Guided Access: the real lock

Baby Mode locks its own screen. Guided Access locks the phone. It is built into iOS, it is free, and while it is on, the phone physically cannot leave the app — no Home swipe, no app switcher, no Control Centre, no notifications pulled down, no accidental calls. It takes about forty seconds to set up and you only do it once.

Set it up (once)

  1. Open Settings › Accessibility › Guided Access
  2. Turn Guided Access on.
  3. Tap Passcode Settings, then Set Guided Access Passcode. Enter a four-digit code twice. Make it different from your phone passcode if a child has ever watched you unlock the phone.
  4. On the same screen you can turn on Face ID (or Touch ID) to end a session with your face instead of the code. Convenient, and safe — a child’s face will not end it.
  5. Scroll down and turn on Accessibility Shortcut. This is what makes the triple-click work.

Start a session

  1. Open Baby Mode and choose a playground.
  2. Triple-click the side button — the long button on the right. On older iPhones with a Home button, triple-click the Home button instead.
  3. If a menu of accessibility options appears, choose Guided Access. (To stop the menu appearing, leave Guided Access as the only shortcut enabled.)
  4. Tap Start, top right. The screen flashes “Guided Access Started”.
  5. Hand over the phone. It is now a toy and nothing else.

End a session

  1. Triple-click the side button again. (With Face ID enabled for Guided Access, double-click the side button and look at the phone.)
  2. Enter your Guided Access passcode.
  3. Tap End, top left.

Worth knowing: on the Guided Access start screen you can circle areas of the screen with your finger to disable them, and tap Options to switch off the volume buttons, the sleep/wake button, keyboards, and motion. Turning off the volume buttons is the difference between a calm car journey and a very loud one.

Baby Mode cannot turn Guided Access on for you. No app can — iOS reserves it for you and your passcode. We show you where the switch is. You flick it. If you forget the Guided Access passcode, Apple’s only remedy is erasing and restoring the phone, so write it down somewhere that is not the phone.

03 There is no sound

Almost always, this is the phone doing exactly what you told it to.

Baby Mode follows the phone’s mute switch. This is deliberate. An app that overrides silent mode is an app that shouts a letter of the alphabet across a quiet restaurant, a waiting room, or a sleeping second child. We decided we would rather you had to flick a switch than have that happen once.

Work down this list

  1. The mute switch. On the top left edge. If you can see an orange stripe, the phone is silenced. Flick it towards the screen.
  2. The Action button. On iPhone 15 Pro and later there is no switch — the button above the volume keys does the job, if it is still set to Silent Mode. Press and hold it, or check Settings › Action Button
  3. The volume. Press volume up while Baby Mode is open, so you are changing the media volume rather than the ringer.
  4. Sound inside Baby Mode. Leave the playground (both bottom corners, three seconds), open the parent settings, and check that Sound is on.
  5. A Focus. Sleep Focus and some Do Not Disturb setups silence media. Check the Control Centre.
  6. Bluetooth. The audio may be going to a car, a speaker in another room, or headphones down the back of the sofa. Look at the top-right of the Control Centre.
  7. Still nothing? Play a track in Music. If that is silent too, it is the phone, not us — restart it.

04 The age bands

Three bands. They change how fast things move, how big the shapes are, how loud it is, and whether anything is spoken aloud. Every playground is available in every band — the band is a pace, not a gate. They are a difficulty setting, chosen by you. Baby Mode does not verify anybody’s age, does not ask for a birthday, and does not care whether you tell it the truth.

BandWhat changes
Under 1Slowest movement, largest shapes, quietest sounds. Nothing is spoken aloud.
1 to 2Middle speed and size, sounds a little louder. Nothing is spoken aloud.
2 to 4Faster, smaller shapes, full volume — and the only band that says letters, numbers and shape names out loud.

Change it any time in the parent settings. Nothing is lost, and the stats keep counting across bands. If the youngest band still feels busy, turn Sound off — it changes the room more than any other setting.

05 Session length

Five, fifteen or thirty minutes. When the time is up, the playground closes on its own and the phone returns to the picker. The screen ends it, so you do not have to be the one who ends it.

The time remaining is shown small along the top edge, for you rather than for them. There is no alert and no countdown noise; over the last ten seconds the screen simply fades to paper, so handing it back is their idea too.

Set it in the parent settings. There is no unlimited option, and there will not be one.

06 Cancelling, trials and refunds

What you are paying for

Baby Mode is free to download and opens with a 7-day free trial of the whole app. After that it is $4.99 per month, renewing automatically until you cancel. There is no cut-down free version — the trial is the free version, and nothing is held back during it.

One subscription, both parents

Family Sharing is on. One subscription covers you and up to five other people in your Apple Family group — six phones and iPads in total, one payment. This is the normal case for us: the phone that gets handed over is whichever one is nearest.

  1. Set up an Apple Family group if you do not have one: Settings › [your name] › Family Sharing
  2. The other parent installs Baby Mode from the App Store on their own device.
  3. They open it signed in to their own Apple Account. It unlocks by itself — there is no code to type and no account to make.

Only the person who pays sees the subscription in their Settings, and only they can cancel it. If you would rather not share this particular subscription, you can switch it off for Baby Mode alone in Settings › [your name] › Subscriptions without leaving the family group.

Cancel the subscription or the trial

Cancelling is done in iOS, not in our app. Apple does not let developers cancel subscriptions on a customer’s behalf, which is annoying for exactly two minutes and reassuring forever.

  1. Open Settings on the iPhone.
  2. Tap your name at the very top.
  3. Tap Subscriptions
  4. Tap Baby Mode.
  5. Tap Cancel Subscription (or Cancel Free Trial), then confirm.

The full path: Settings › [your name] › Subscriptions › Baby Mode › Cancel Subscription

You can also get there from the App Store: open the App Store, tap your profile picture top right, tap Subscriptions.

Cancel at least 24 hours before the trial ends or the first charge goes through. Apple bills a day early on some accounts. If you are trying it out and are not sure, cancel on day five — you keep full access until day seven either way. Cancelling never cuts you off immediately; it stops the next charge and leaves you access to the end of the period you have paid for.

If you were charged and did not mean to be

Apple is the merchant. Every payment, receipt and refund goes through them, and they are quick about it. Go to reportaproblem.apple.com, sign in with your Apple Account, find Baby Mode and request a refund. Say plainly that you did not intend to subscribe. Most of these are granted.

We cannot issue refunds ourselves — we never see your payment details and cannot touch the transaction. If Apple declines and you think that is wrong, email us and we will tell you honestly whether we think you have a case.

07 Restoring a purchase

New phone, restored backup, deleted and reinstalled the app, or a second phone in the house — the subscription follows the Apple Account, not the device.

  1. Open Baby Mode. You will be looking at the paywall.
  2. Tap Restore Purchases, at the bottom.
  3. Confirm with Face ID, Touch ID or your Apple Account password if asked.

Nothing is charged. Nothing can be charged twice — iOS will not let it happen.

If Restore does nothing, check three things, in this order:

  • You are signed in with the same Apple Account that bought it. Look in Settings › [your name] — on a shared family phone this is the usual culprit.
  • The subscription is still active: Settings › [your name] › Subscriptions
  • The phone is online. Restoring is the one thing in Baby Mode that needs a network, because it has to ask Apple.

The subscription belongs to the Apple Account that bought it, and works on any iPhone or iPad signed into that account.

08 Questions people actually ask

My child got out. How?

Almost certainly by swiping up from the bottom of the screen to go Home — babies press their whole palm on the glass and produce gestures no adult would think of. Baby Mode cannot block the system Home gesture. Nothing on the App Store can. That is what Guided Access is for; see section 02.

Can they call someone, or send a message?

Not from inside Baby Mode — there is no dialer, no contacts access and no way to reach another app from within a playground. But if they swipe out to the Home Screen, they are back in a normal iPhone with normal apps. Guided Access is the only thing that makes that impossible. Set it up once.

Will notifications still come through?

Yes. Baby Mode cannot suppress notifications; that is a system setting. Turn on a Focus, or switch on Guided Access, which prevents the notification centre being pulled down even when banners arrive.

Does it need the internet?

No. Every playground, every setting and the stats screen work in Aeroplane Mode, in a tunnel, on a plane, in a car park, at a wedding. The only thing that needs a network is the initial purchase and the Restore Purchases button. There is no download, no streaming and no sync.

Do you collect anything about my child?

No. Baby Mode makes no network calls at all. No account, no ads, no analytics, no tracking, no third-party code. Settings and play counts live on the phone and are deleted with the app. Our App Store privacy label says “Data Not Collected”. The full privacy policy spells out COPPA, CCPA and GDPR in detail. There is nothing to collect and nowhere to send it.

What is the stats screen for?

It counts how long each playground was open and how many taps it got, so you can see which toy actually gets reached for. It is for you, it is stored on your phone, and it goes nowhere. It is not a developmental assessment and should not be read as one.

Is there an iPad version?

Yes. Baby Mode is one app that runs on both. On iPad the playgrounds fill the larger screen with more to touch rather than the same thing scaled up, the corner targets grow and sit further apart, and the screen rotates. Your subscription covers both.

Can I use it on two phones?

Yes, in two ways. On any iPhone or iPad you sign into with your own Apple Account, the subscription follows you — see section 07. And because Family Sharing is on, up to five other people in your Apple Family group can use it on their own accounts, off the one payment — see section 06.

The screen keeps dimming and locking.

Baby Mode keeps the screen awake while a playground is open. If it still dims, check Settings › Display & Brightness › Auto-Lock and try switching Low Power Mode off.

Will it rotate to landscape?

On iPhone, correct — it is portrait only, because a screen that rotates in a small pair of hands is one more thing to go wrong. iPad rotates freely, and a drawing survives the rotation rather than being wiped.

I reinstalled and my settings are gone.

Expected. Settings live on the device and only on the device, so deleting the app removes them. That is the same decision as collecting nothing — there is no cloud copy because there is no cloud. Setting it up again takes about fifteen seconds. Your subscription is unaffected; tap Restore Purchases.

Is the screen time bad for them?

We are not going to pretend an app is a health intervention. What we will say is what the app does: it does not autoplay video, it has no infinite feed, no rewards, no streaks, no notifications and no way to get to anything else. It stops on its own after five, fifteen or thirty minutes. If you want zero screen time, that is a completely reasonable position and you do not need this app.

Can you add a playground?

Possibly. Tell us what your child actually does with the phone when they get hold of it — that is more useful to us than a feature request.

Accessibility

Baby Mode respects Reduce Motion, the system volume and the mute switch, and it scales the text on every parent-facing screen with your text size setting. It does not scale type inside the playgrounds, because there the letters and numerals are the artwork rather than reading matter. The exit gesture is labelled for VoiceOver. If a specific setting makes it unusable for you, email us — we read every one of these.
